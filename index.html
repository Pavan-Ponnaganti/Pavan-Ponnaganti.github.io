Research

One thread runs through my work: small producers lose value because nobody can trace their goods and they cannot sell at scale.

01 Trace

Know where a batch has been

Blockchain custody records point to the exact party behind a fault, without exposing the consumer who reported it.

02 Aggregate

Sell together

Clustering and matching let many small producers act like one larger seller and reach more distant buyers.

03 Deploy

Test it in a real mandi

STACLI runs these ideas with working traders, in a live pilot with 12 traders.

Traceability with accountability

VITAL models each batch as a custody tree on a blockchain. When a complaint arrives, it ranks the custody nodes by how likely each caused the fault, and reports whether the failure was at a single node, a region, or the whole batch. Consumers file complaints with zero-knowledge proofs, so no identity is stored.

AutoVITAL removes the trusted regional auditor from that design and handles batches where several parties failed at once.

Aggregation and market intelligence

A smallholder alone has little bargaining power and a short selling radius. I study how grouping farmers by location and produce, then matching the groups to buyers, can deliver economies of scale and widen the market each farmer can reach. The matching foundations come from my work on many-to-many matching and on distributed matching algorithms.
